# Independent live-site visual review

Reviewed only http://127.0.0.1:5176/ in Chromium at 1440 × 900 and 390 × 844. No project source, project documents, previous reviews, or other reviewers were consulted. Screenshots in this directory were produced during this review; filenames desktop-top.png, desktop-hero-before.png and capture.cjs pre-existed and were not used.

**Weighted overall: 7.9 / 10.** The opening is memorable, confident and materially more distinctive than a standard studio portfolio. The full journey is clean and usable, but the project and people sections do not sustain the opening's level of authored visual surprise or demonstrate enough software craft to make the whole experience exceptional against an Awwwards-level bar.

## What is working

- **Hero craft:** The large DIET CODE wordmark and bottle overlap have strong tension without losing the supporting studio copy or the OOTD action. The bottle has detailed condensation, printed texture, a cream cutout border and convincing visual mass. It is an actual visual asset, not merely a placeholder. Evidence: desktop-entry.png, mobile-entry.png.
- **Responsive art direction:** The mobile bottle rotates into a diagonal upright composition under a two-line wordmark. The torn-paper studio label and OOTD button remain readable in the first 844 px. The project garment row is deliberately staggered rather than simply shrinking the desktop. Evidence: mobile-entry.png, mobile-forward-600.png.
- **Typography and system:** The oversized display headings, compact labels, fine rules, black/cream fields and repeated asterisk produce a consistent voice. The studio-to-contact transition and red footer contact area close the visual loop established by the hero. Evidence: desktop-forward-2250.png, desktop-forward-2992.png, mobile-forward-3000.png.
- **Project states:** Wardrobe, Today and Looks each change the panel imagery, background treatment, headline, explanation and process labels. These are meaningful content states. Evidence: desktop-forward-1350.png, desktop-tab-Today.png, desktop-tab-Looks.png, mobile-looks-detail.png.
- **Real interaction checks:** Work navigated to #work; Studio to #studio; Contact to #contact; Back to top returned to y=0. Mobile menu opened, closed after choosing Studio, and also closed with Escape. Keyboard skip navigation focused H1#hero-title. ArrowRight moved from Wardrobe to Today and changed aria-selected with a strong visible red focus outline. Evidence: desktop-navigation-Work.png, desktop-navigation-Studio.png, desktop-navigation-Contact.png, desktop-keyboard-tab-final.png, mobile-menu.png.

## Defects and highest-value improvements

1. **The project section sells the wardrobe concept more convincingly than the digital product.** All three states show clothing arrangements and editorial labels; none exposes a real app screen, an interaction or a piece of product behavior. This is a particularly material gap for a creative engineering studio. Add one carefully composed actual interface sequence once available, while keeping the current honest “in development” status. Do not invent product evidence. Evidence: desktop-tab-Today.png, desktop-tab-Looks.png, mobile-looks-detail.png.

2. **A tiny bottle fragment survives the hero exit.** Near the end of the desktop paper reveal, a narrow green/cream sliver remains in the upper-right cream field while the main hero has essentially disappeared. It looks like residual clipping rather than a deliberate final pose. It occurs in both forward and reverse samples. Finish the reveal by clearing the bottle/cap silhouette completely or retaining an intentional larger visible shape. Evidence: desktop-forward-450.png, desktop-reverse-450.png, motion-forward-400.png. The screenshot labels identify captures, not a promise that animation-dependent scroll position equals the filename number.

3. **The strongest visual idea is concentrated at the top.** Below the bottle the site largely becomes fixed garment compositions, outlined panels, big headlines and ruled name rows. These are well executed, but the studio section in particular offers only names, aliases and asterisks. Add one purposeful authored moment in the work or people section: a product interaction, distinct team artwork, or an image treatment tied to how the studio works. Avoid adding decorative motion merely to increase activity. Evidence: desktop-forward-1350.png, desktop-forward-2250.png.

4. **The people section has little individual substance.** “Gowrish / aka gorix”, “Jeswin / aka jes” and “Sabi / aka sero” establish the crew but do not explain what each person brings. A short specific capability or useful link per person would make the hiring invitation more credible and give the typographic rows more purpose. Evidence: desktop-navigation-Studio.png, mobile-forward-2400.png.

5. **Some mobile information is visually very small.** The hero's lower metadata, garment captions, numbered labels and process labels sit near the lower comfortable reading range at 390 px. The main narrative is readable; the secondary layer requires more effort than the bold headings. Increase or simplify the least essential microcopy where it improves reading without adding visual noise. Evidence: mobile-entry.png, mobile-forward-600.png, mobile-looks-detail.png.

6. **Arrow meaning is somewhat overloaded.** The same northeast arrow means a tab switch, contact action and an editorial arrow beside “Indigo travel layers.” The latter reads as an invitation to open a saved look, but the rendered control inventory does not expose it as a link or button. Make the intended action explicit, or remove the action-like arrow from a purely illustrative heading. Evidence: desktop-tab-Looks.png, mobile-looks-detail.png. This is an affordance critique, not a claim that an undisclosed destination is broken.

## Motion and coverage detail

The review included timed live captures of the cap before and after activation. The cap visibly lifts, separates and rotates away from the mouth; the label changes to “Pop it again.” Forward/reverse wheel samples through the hero show the bottle changing pose as the cream torn-paper edge advances and recedes. The reveal reverses into the initial composition rather than leaving the page empty. Evidence: desktop-cap-100.png, desktop-cap-400.png, desktop-cap-900.png; motion-forward-100.png through motion-forward-400.png; motion-reverse-300.png through motion-reverse-0.png. The mobile cap state was also checked. With prefers-reduced-motion enabled, the cap had reached the same final pose at 100 ms and 700 ms rather than continuing the ordinary animated flight; evidence: mobile-reduced-cap-100.png, mobile-reduced-cap-700.png.

Whole-page coverage: hero; OOTD overview; project explanation; all three project panels; studio; contact; footer. Forward and reverse scrolling were exercised at both requested sizes. Desktop navigation, mobile menu, tab switching and selected keyboard interactions were exercised. Mobile document width and scrollWidth were both 390, with initial document height 3762 px.

Limits: Chromium headless at the two specified viewports only. Visual motion was judged from live timed browser captures and interaction outcomes; this is not a measured FPS/performance audit or a substitute for a physical phone. No screen reader, browser zoom/reflow matrix, full contrast audit, Safari/Firefox, email delivery or external destination flow was tested. The reduced-motion check covered the cap and initial/mobile navigation behavior, not every animation path. No source changes were made.
